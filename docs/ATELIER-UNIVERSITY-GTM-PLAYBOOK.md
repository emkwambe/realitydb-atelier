## The single most important insight

<cite index="13-1">In EdTech, a pilot is not just a trial. It is often a political safety mechanism,
a proof-building process, an adoption test, and an internal justification tool.</cite>

Universities do not buy software. They adopt it — slowly, through relationships,
with internal champions who need political cover and evidence to bring to committees.

Every element of Atelier's university GTM is designed around this reality.

## Who the buyer actually is (the three-layer model)

University EdTech purchases involve three distinct decision-makers.
You must engage all three but in the right sequence.

**Layer 1 — The Faculty Champion (your entry point)**
An individual professor who assigns Atelier in their course.
They are not the buyer. They are the proof generator.
A faculty champion who runs a successful pilot creates the evidence
that moves the institutional purchase forward.

**Target profile:** Associate or full professor teaching analytics,
business intelligence, data-driven decision making, or FP&A courses.
Research-active preferred — they understand evidence and want to
produce it. 15-40 students per course. Some comfort with
technology-integrated assessment.

**What they care about:**
- Does this make my course better without making my life harder?
- Can I defend the assessment to students who dispute grades?
- Will this give me data I can use in a curriculum committee presentation?
- Does it satisfy AACSB Assurance of Learning requirements?

**Layer 2 — The Department Champion (your expansion path)**
A department chair, curriculum director, or program director who
sees Atelier across multiple courses. They approve the institutional
license after the faculty pilot proves value.

**What they care about:**
- AACSB AoL compliance documentation
- Learning outcomes data for accreditation reports
- Cost relative to alternatives (Capsim: $10-20/student; Markstrat: $30-50/student)
- Faculty adoption rate (will colleagues use it or resist it?)

**Layer 3 — Procurement (your finishing move)**
<cite index="21-1">From faculty and administrative staff identifying needs to procurement officers
managing sourcing, negotiation, and vendor relationships, having skilled and
informed personnel is essential for smooth operations.</cite>

Procurement enters only after Layers 1 and 2 have validated the purchase.
They care about: vendor stability, data privacy compliance, contract terms,
and whether the price fits under the no-bid threshold.

**The no-bid threshold is critical:** Most universities can approve purchases
under $10,000-$15,000 without a formal RFP. Atelier's semester license
($4,999) and annual license ($14,999) are designed to stay under this
threshold at most institutions. A department chair can approve these
without triggering procurement review.

## The competitive landscape

### Capsim — the closest comparable

<cite index="23-1">Capsim offers competitive, team-based simulations where student teams manage
virtual companies across R&D, marketing, production, and finance. With 1.7 million+
users across 1,100+ institutions, it is the market leader for undergraduate and MBA
strategy courses. Their instructor support materials and assessment alignment tools
are among the most developed in the space.</cite>

**Capsim's weaknesses that Atelier exploits:**
- No AI-graded individual assessment — Capsim measures team decisions, not individual judgment
- No data analysis component — students make slider decisions, not SQL investigations
- No verifiable individual credential — team completion, not individual evidence
- Fixed scenario — same companies, same market structure every time
- <cite index="26-1">The interface retains a spreadsheet feel, and there is no AI-driven layer for adaptive scenarios. Customization options are limited.</cite>

**The Atelier positioning against Capsim:**
"Capsim measures whether your team can run a simulated business.
Atelier measures whether you can diagnose what is wrong with a real one
and brief the executive team on what to do about it."

### Forage — the career-prep adjacent

<cite index="34-1">EAB's Forage job simulation platform has exceeded 10 million student engagements.
The Forage team works with top employers such as Citibank, Goldman Sachs, KPMG,
Red Bull, and BCG to design virtual job simulations that give students a
"day in the life" preview of early-career job roles.</cite>

**Forage's weaknesses that Atelier exploits:**
- Employer-authored content (BCG, Goldman) — not faculty-curated curriculum
- Career exploration, not academic assessment — no grades, no AoL evidence
- No SQL investigation environment — surface-level task simulation
- No AI grading or competency measurement — completion only

**The Atelier positioning against Forage:**
"Forage shows students what a job looks like.
Atelier proves they can do the work."

### Markstrat / other simulations

<cite index="26-1">Markstrat is the gold standard for marketing strategy simulation, with over 30 years
of use at INSEAD, Wharton, and dozens of leading schools. The trade-off: it is
instructor-dependent, requiring significant facilitation skill. There is no
self-service authoring, and the platform does not extend beyond marketing.
Typically $30-50+ per student.</cite>

**Atelier's positioning against domain-specific simulations:**
"Markstrat teaches marketing strategy. Capsim teaches operations strategy.
Atelier teaches the analytical judgment that applies across all of them —
the ability to interrogate data and brief leadership on what it means."

## The AACSB hook — the institutional unlock

This is the single most powerful selling point with department chairs
and deans. It requires understanding before using.

<cite index="42-1">Under Standard 5 of AACSB's Business Accreditation Standards, schools must have
"well-documented assurance of learning processes" and produce evidence that these
processes lead to curricular improvements. Nearly 68 percent of institutions
seeking AACSB accreditation were identified by peer review teams as having
shortcomings in their AoL systems.</cite>

<cite index="44-1">Schools are advised to provide more robust evidence of how they are "closing the loop"
in their AoL processes by actively using assessment data to inform and drive improvements.
This involves refining the execution of AoL plans and clearly demonstrating the specific
improvements that have been implemented based on assessment outcomes.</cite>

**What this means for Atelier:**

Atelier is one of the only tools available that produces AACSB AoL evidence
automatically. Every student submission generates:
- Per-competency scores mapped to AACSB learning goals
- Cohort-level competency analytics (the heatmap)
- Benchmark comparison (this cohort vs Atelier global)
- A formatted outcomes report ready for the curriculum committee

**The selling sentence for department chairs:**

> "Atelier produces AACSB Assurance of Learning evidence automatically.
> Every semester you run it, you get a competency analytics report you
> can submit directly to your AoL documentation. No rubric-building,
> no manual data collection, no separate assessment instrument to maintain."

<cite index="42-1">The good news is that 2025's technology toolkit offers a way out of this rut.
AI and smart systems can transform AoL from a compliance chore into an
intelligent, streamlined process.</cite>

Atelier is that tool for data acumen in business analytics programs.

## The academic calendar — when to sell

<cite index="19-1">Educational purchasing follows predictable annual cycles that dramatically impact
GTM strategy. Launch timing matters more in education than almost any other sector.</cite>

<cite index="16-1">Higher education provides higher ACVs but with extended cycles.</cite>

**The university buying calendar:**

| Month | Activity | What Atelier should be doing |
|---|---|---|
| August-September | Faculty prep for fall semester | Outreach to faculty for spring pilot conversations |
| October-November | Mid-semester | Pilot faculty running first cohort — check-in email |
| November-December | Budget planning for next year | Department chair conversations — license for spring |
| December | End-of-semester chaos | Nothing — do not send anything |
| January | Spring semester start | Pilot cohorts begin for spring |
| February-March | AACSB AoL data collection season | Share AoL value prop with deans |
| April-May | End of spring semester | Outcomes reports generated, debrief calls |
| May-June | Summer — curriculum committee season | Reference case conversations, next fall planning |
| July | Faculty availability window | Best time for product demos and pilot agreements |

**The critical insight:**
<cite index="13-1">Budget cycles, academic calendars, pilots, procurement rules, grant windows,
board approvals, and implementation seasons all shape how buyers move.</cite>

The semester license structure ($4,999/semester) aligns with how universities
budget — semester by semester — rather than requiring an annual commitment
upfront. Start with semester, convert to annual after two semesters of data.

### Stage 2 — Proof generation (Spring 2027)

**What happens:** 3-5 pilot cohorts complete at least one module.

**The proof package this creates:**

### Stage 3 — Reference-led expansion (Summer-Fall 2027)

**Target:** 10-20 institutions in the second wave

**Channel:** Reference cases from Stage 2 pilots + conference presence

**The conference play:**
<cite index="20-1">Winning GTM teams are shifting from feature-driven messaging to proof-led storytelling.
This includes learning efficacy data, retention and completion metrics, and
demonstrated improvements in academic or workforce performance.</cite>

Target conferences where business analytics faculty gather:
- **INFORMS Annual Meeting** (October) — operations research and analytics faculty
- **Decision Sciences Institute** (November) — business school faculty
- **AACSB Deans Conference** (February) — department chairs and deans
- **AIS SIGED** (December) — information systems educators

At each conference: present a 20-minute session on "Measuring Decision Intelligence
in Business Analytics Curricula" — not a product demo, a research presentation
using the pilot data. This is how Capsim built credibility: faculty-to-faculty
at academic conferences, not sales-to-buyer.

**Goal:** 10 institutional licenses signed by December 2027.

---

### Stage 4 — Scalable channel (2028)

**Target:** 50+ institutions

**Channel:** Three parallel tracks:

**Track A — LMS integration partners**
<cite index="23-1">Single sign-on and gradebook sync with Canvas, Blackboard, and the rest —
students launch straight from your course, and grades flow back on their own.</cite>

Canvas and Blackboard integration makes Atelier adoptable without IT involvement.
Students find it in their course shell. Grades sync automatically. Instructors do not
need to manage a separate platform. This removes the largest adoption friction point.

**Track B — Textbook publisher partnership (Forage model)**
<cite index="30-1">Pearson and Forage partnered to embed job simulations into Pearson's MyLab
courseware platform. Millions of students use MyLab each year to access their
coursework and eTextbooks.</cite>

The equivalent for Atelier: partner with a business analytics textbook publisher
(Cengage, Pearson, Wiley) to bundle Atelier access with their analytics textbook.
Students who buy the textbook get Atelier access included. The publisher's sales
team sells Atelier for free to every instructor who adopts their textbook.

**Track C — Consortium licensing**
Sell annual licenses to university systems (UNC system, CSU system) rather than
individual campuses. One contract, 10-20 campuses, centralized procurement.
Requires enterprise pricing and LMS integration but dramatically reduces
per-institution sales cost.

### Stage 5 — Category ownership (2029+)

At sufficient scale (100+ institutions, 50,000+ student completions),

The IES credential is what enables this. A validated, published assessment

## The sales conversation — what to say at each level

### With a faculty member (Layer 1)

**Opening:**
"I built Atelier after 12 years teaching analytics. The problem I kept seeing
was students who could write correct SQL but couldn't tell a CFO what it meant.
I want to show you what a 30-minute investigation looks like and get your reaction."

**The demo sequence (20 minutes):**
1. Show the NovaPay scenario card (2 minutes) — the hook, the role, the 72-hour clock
2. Run Exercise 3 live (5 minutes) — show the SQL workbench, run a cohort query
3. Show the ADAI grader in action (3 minutes) — submit a generic briefing, show the 59
4. Show the instructor dashboard (5 minutes) — progress grid, override form, answer keys
5. Show the outcomes report (3 minutes) — the AACSB AoL document
6. Discuss fit for their course (2 minutes) — what content, what semester

**The close:**
"I am running a structured pilot program for Spring 2027. No cost during the pilot.
You get full instructor dashboard access, I get a 30-minute debrief call at the end.
If it works for your students, we talk about continuing. If not, nothing lost."

**Never say:**
- "We have an enterprise plan for your institution" — too fast, wrong audience
- "Let me send you a proposal" — signals a sales process, kills the relationship
- "We integrate with Canvas" — not relevant until they have decided to adopt

---

### With a department chair (Layer 2)

**Opening:**
"[Faculty name] ran Atelier in [Course name] last semester and asked me to
connect with you. The pilot generated some data I thought would be useful
for your AoL documentation — can I share it?"

**The conversation:**
1. Show the outcomes report from the faculty pilot (2 minutes)
2. Explain the AACSB AoL connection — competency mapping, closing the loop (3 minutes)
3. Show the benchmark comparison — their cohort vs global Atelier average (2 minutes)
4. Discuss the institutional license — semester ($4,999) or annual ($14,999) (5 minutes)
5. Discuss which other courses might benefit (5 minutes)

**The close:**
"The semester license is $4,999 for up to 30 students per course, up to 3 courses
per semester. That is under your standard approval threshold. [Faculty name] can
sign off, or if you prefer I can work through your standard vendor process."

**The AACSB angle in one sentence:**
"Every semester you run Atelier, the instructor dashboard generates an AoL
evidence report you can submit directly to your accreditation documentation
without building a separate rubric or collecting data manually."

### With procurement (Layer 3)

By the time procurement is involved, the purchase decision is already made.
Procurement needs:
- A vendor information form (prepare this in advance)
- Data privacy documentation (FERPA compliance, privacy policy URL)
- A W-9
- A contract or purchase order

**Pre-prepared documents needed:**
- Vendor information sheet (company name, EIN, address, banking)
- FERPA compliance statement (one paragraph — data handling, no PII shared)
- Standard license agreement (one page, plain language)
- W-9 on file

## Pricing architecture for universities

| Tier | Price | Coverage | Approval path |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faculty pilot | $0 | 1 course, 1 semester, ≤40 students | Pilot agreement only |
| Semester license | $4,999 | Up to 3 courses, 1 semester, ≤30 students/course | Department chair approval |
| Annual license | $14,999 | Up to 6 courses, 2 semesters, ≤30 students/course | Department or dean approval |
| Program license | $29,999/year | Unlimited courses, 1 degree program | Dean approval |
| Institutional license | Custom | All programs, campus-wide | Provost / procurement |

**Pricing design principles:**
- Semester license stays under $5,000 — below no-bid threshold at most institutions
- Annual license stays under $15,000 — one-person approval at most programs
- Per-student pricing never shown — it signals "course material" not "assessment instrument"
- Competitor reference: <cite index="26-1">Capsim approximately $10-20 per student; Markstrat $30-50+ per student</cite>
  At 30 students, Atelier semester = $167/student. Framed as an instrument, not priced per student.

## What Atelier is NOT selling to universities

Do not position Atelier as:
- A replacement for case study teaching (HBS cases are trusted, Atelier is supplementary)
- A full course management system (Canvas, Blackboard handle this — Atelier integrates)
- A SQL training tool (that is SQL Learn — wrong product, wrong audience)
- A gamified learning platform (DataCamp, Codecademy — consumer positioning)
- An AI tutoring system (different category entirely)

Position Atelier as:
- An assessment instrument for data acumen (the ADAI)
- A structured investigation environment for business judgment
- A source of AACSB AoL evidence
- A credential system that names what students demonstrated

## The IES grant as a sales accelerator

Once the IES Phase IB proposal is submitted (target: April 2027), every
university conversation changes. "We are conducting a federally funded
research study on decision intelligence assessment" signals:
- This is not a startup selling software — it is a research program
- The assessment instrument is being externally validated
- Participating institutions become co-investigators, not just customers
- Data from the pilot contributes to the research — faculty can publish from it

This transforms the pilot from a product trial into a research participation.
Universities that are reluctant to be "sold to" are often eager to "participate in research."

The IES framing works especially well with:
- Research universities with faculty who need publication opportunities
- Institutions building analytics programs that want to differentiate
- Business schools facing AACSB AoL deficiencies who need a documented solution
