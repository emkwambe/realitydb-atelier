### Email 2 — Pre-semester briefing (one week before course start)

**Subject:** Before your students start — three things worth knowing

---

Hi [Name],

Your course starts [date]. Three things that will save you time during the semester:

**1. The progress grid is your main tool**

Once students enroll, your progress grid at https://atelier.realitydb.dev/instructor/cohorts/[id]/progress shows exactly where each student stands — not started, in progress, submitted, passed, below pass. You do not need to chase students for updates. The grid tells you who is stuck before they tell you.

A flag will appear next to any student who has been on the same exercise for 5+ days without advancing. That is your early warning signal.

**2. The competency heatmap becomes useful after the first 5-6 submissions**

Once enough students have submitted, the analytics tab shows you which competency dimensions your cohort is strong on and which need attention. In most cohorts, Executive Communication and Data Fidelity are where students struggle most — the grid will tell you if your cohort follows that pattern or breaks it.

Use this to calibrate your in-class discussion. If the heatmap shows weak causal reasoning across the cohort, that is a discussion topic worth spending 20 minutes on.

**3. The grade export works with Canvas and Blackboard**

When you are ready to record grades, the export tab generates a CSV that imports directly into Canvas, Blackboard, and D2L. The columns match the standard gradebook format. If your LMS is something else, send me the required format and I will make sure the export matches it.

One more thing: at the end of the semester, the outcomes report at https://atelier.realitydb.dev/instructor/cohorts/[id]/exports generates a two-page PDF showing your cohort's competency results compared to the Atelier global benchmark. Some instructors have found this useful for curriculum committee presentations.

If anything comes up before the semester starts, I am at this address.

— Eddy

## Pilot program operations — internal notes

### Target faculty profile

The ideal first pilot faculty member has:
- An analytics, data science, or business intelligence course with 15-40 students
- Some comfort with technology-integrated assessment
- Institutional credibility (associate or full professor preferred over adjunct)
- Interest in research (makes the debrief call more productive)
- At a research university or strong regional university (their name carries weight)

**First targets (Eddy's existing network):**
- Brandeis University analytics faculty (MS Strategic Analytics program — personal connection)
- ECPI University data science faculty (former employer — warm introduction possible)
- UNC Charlotte business analytics faculty (local — in-person meeting possible)
- NC State Poole College of Management (strong analytics program, 45 minutes away)

### Pilot capacity for first semester

Target: 3-5 pilot cohorts for Spring 2027.
Reason: enough data for the IES proposal human vs AI scoring study,
manageable support load for a solo founder, diverse enough institutions
to make a credible research claim.

### Success criteria for each pilot

A pilot is successful if:
1. ≥ 70% of enrolled students submit at least one briefing
2. The instructor completes the debrief call
3. The instructor would recommend Atelier to a colleague (ask directly in the debrief)
4. Anonymized data is available for the IES scoring comparison study

A pilot becomes a reference customer if the instructor presents outcomes
at a department meeting or curriculum committee — ask them to do this
during the debrief call.
